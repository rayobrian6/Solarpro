# Fixture Intelligence Analysis and Credit-Efficiency Review V1

Geometry Intelligence V1 was validated against 16 expanded professional survey fixtures and targeted geometry intelligence torture cases. The expanded fixture runtime metrics were saved in `geometry-intelligence-fixture-metrics-v1.json`. The fixture corpus includes clean roof geometry, missing pitch, missing azimuth, duplicate roof planes, self-intersecting polygons, obstruction-only data, ground mount, solar fence, conflicting panel count, mixed unit issues, incomplete electrical service, meter/MSP mismatches, CAD preview gaps, document-derived partial evidence, and geometry-ready-without-preview behavior.

The expanded fixture distribution showed 8 low-risk fixtures, 4 moderate-risk fixtures, and 4 critical fixtures. Review urgency distribution was 8 none, 4 routine_review, and 4 blocker_review. The risk category counts were overlapping_geometry 1, low_confidence_geometry 3, near_zero_geometry_projections 1, readiness_downgrade_conditions 7, unsupported_polygon_structures 1, and conflicting_survey_evidence 4. Readiness downgrade patterns appeared in 7 fixtures.

The confidence distributions show useful separation without overfitting. Geometry confidence ranged from 30 to 100 with an average of 89.13. Topology integrity ranged from 40 to 100 with an average of 95.06. Readiness trust ranged from 13 to 100 with an average of 76.81. Discrepancy severity ranged from 0 to 18 with an average of 1.13. Clean fixtures remain high-trust, while malformed and incomplete fixtures are distinguishable through risk and urgency classifications.

Targeted intelligence tests extend the expanded fixtures with overlap, duplicate edge, near-zero projection, false-positive tracking, and missing evidence downgrade cases. This approach was credit-conscious: instead of adding many new large fixtures, the phase reused existing expanded fixtures and added only compact targeted cases for behaviors that required precise geometry construction.

Implementation scope efficiency was high. The phase added one reusable intelligence module, one focused test file, one compact readiness-report integration, one generated fixture metrics artifact, and four reports. It avoided broad parser refactors, new persistence schemas, large UI work, additional OSS dependencies, ML/AI systems, OpenCV/OCR integrations, CAD authority changes, and speculative abstraction layers.

Reusable primitives added include deterministic geometry trust scoring, topology integrity scoring, readiness trust scoring, discrepancy severity scoring, risk classification, review urgency classification, risk signals, discrepancy clustering, false-positive/false-negative tracking, and lightweight operator summary generation. These primitives can be reused by future reporting, operator review, CAD preview gating analysis, audit dashboards, or offline fixture replay without becoming authoritative.

Future leverage gained is significant relative to implementation scope. SolarPro now has a scoring layer that can explain geometry trustworthiness across native parser evidence, canonical geometry, readiness artifacts, and isolated OSS comparison outputs. This creates a foundation for future CAD authority phases, but the architecture should not promote authority yet. The system is becoming mature enough to inform future CAD authority design because it now has deterministic DTOs, readiness hashes, comparison boundaries, discrepancy reports, and trust scoring. However, production CAD authority should still wait for broader corpus replay, explicit human review workflows, stronger native overlap/duplicate-plane policy, and obstruction polygon DTO support if obstruction topology becomes important.

Estimated architectural value per implementation scope is high because the phase converted existing infrastructure into reusable intelligence without major framework churn. The main complexity avoided was creating another authority path. The main leverage gained was explainability: operators and future engineering systems can now see not only whether geometry is ready, but how trustworthy it is, why it may be risky, what entities are affected, and what review action is recommended.
