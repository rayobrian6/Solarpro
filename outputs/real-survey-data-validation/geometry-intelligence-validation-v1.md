# Geometry Intelligence V1 Validation Report

Geometry Intelligence V1 was implemented as a deterministic, read-only, non-authoritative intelligence layer in `lib/siteSurvey/geometryIntelligence.ts`. The layer consumes existing high-leverage artifacts rather than creating a new authority path: `ProfessionalSiteSurveyEvidenceBundleV1`, `CanonicalSurveyGeometryV1`, `SurveyCADReadinessV1`, and the isolated `GeometryComparisonReportV1` from the polygon-clipping comparison adapter. Its output is `GeometryIntelligenceReportV1`, which provides geometry confidence, topology integrity, readiness trust, discrepancy severity, risk classification, review urgency, risk signals, discrepancy clusters, and explainable review actions.

The implementation preserves the project safety boundary. It does not mutate canonical geometry, CAD previews, parser evidence, readiness artifacts, persistence, or production CAD. It does not execute CAD solvers, auto-correct geometry, promote readiness, or introduce ML/AI prediction. The report includes explicit no-authority flags and a `noAuthorityEnforcement` block documenting read-only execution, no canonical geometry mutation, no CAD mutation, no CAD solver execution, no persistence, no readiness promotion, and no engineering authority.

Validation was fixture-driven and deterministic. The focused validation command was `npm test -- lib/siteSurvey/professionalSurveyParser.test.ts lib/siteSurvey/professionalSurveyReadinessReport.test.ts lib/siteSurvey/professionalSurveyExpandedFixtures.test.ts lib/siteSurvey/geometryComparisonAdapter.test.ts lib/siteSurvey/geometryIntelligence.test.ts --reporter=verbose`. The result was 5 test files passed and 55 tests passed. The validation covered parser behavior, readiness behavior, expanded fixture behavior, adapter comparison behavior, and new intelligence behavior. TypeScript was also checked with `npm run type-check` and reported no errors.

Geometry Intelligence V1 tests verify that clean geometry produces stable high-trust scores, deterministic hashes, low risk, and no review urgency. They verify that overlapping roof planes are detected as discrepancy intelligence while native readiness remains unchanged. They verify that self-intersecting geometry remains natively blocked and is classified as critical with blocker review urgency. They verify duplicate edge and near-zero projection behavior as explainable topology degradation. They verify false-positive tracking by comparing bounding-box overlap expectations against polygon-clipping intersection results. They also run across all expanded fixtures to confirm stable confidence distribution behavior.

The expanded fixture metrics file `geometry-intelligence-fixture-metrics-v1.json` was generated from actual runtime output. Across 16 expanded fixtures, geometry confidence scores ranged from 30 to 100 with an average of 89.13, topology integrity ranged from 40 to 100 with an average of 95.06, readiness trust ranged from 13 to 100 with an average of 76.81, and discrepancy severity ranged from 0 to 18 with an average of 1.13. Risk classifications were 8 low, 4 moderate, and 4 critical. Review urgency classifications were 8 none, 4 routine_review, and 4 blocker_review.

The validation confirms that the layer is stable enough to serve as a reusable geometry trust primitive while remaining review-first and non-authoritative. It improves operational insight without changing canonical geometry, readiness authority, CAD previews, persistence, or downstream engineering decisions.
