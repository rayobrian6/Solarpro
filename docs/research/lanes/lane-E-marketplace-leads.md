# Lane E — Marketplace / Leads UX

Research worker lane. Benchmarked against SolarPro's `/network` marketplace (code audited 2026-09-25).
Scope: interaction design of lead/contractor marketplaces, not market sizing.

---

## SUMMARY — transferable interaction principles

1. **Publish the competition count on the lead card, before payment.** Thumbtack: "You can see the number of pros a customer contacts right on the lead." Every failure story in this lane is "I paid, then found 5 others bought it."
2. **A refund policy is a UI, not a page.** Thumbtack enumerates 7 qualifying situations, a 45-day window, a 4-state tracker (Pending/Refunded/Credited/Denied) and a 24–48h SLA. Networx publishes what does *not* qualify. That's the leapfrog.
3. **Some refunds must be automatic.** Thumbtack auto-refunds Trust-&-Safety-flagged leads. Bark's Get Hired Guarantee auto-returns unused first-pack credits 3 days after last purchase. Human-arbitrated-only credit is the #1 documented failure mode.
4. **Verify the phone before you sell it.** Thumbtack's Phone Number Guarantee (identity-matching) converts the industry's loudest complaint into a marketing asset.
5. **Price the lead on behaviour, not just grade.** CraftJack's patent-pending Speed-To-Call: call within 30 minutes → 20% off the lead. Aligns platform revenue with contractor outcome.
6. **Give spend a ceiling and an autopilot.** zolar Autobuy + weekly caps; Thumbtack max lead price slider + weekly budget. Browsing-and-clicking does not scale to a working installer.
7. **Show buyer-side trust facts, not seller-side adjectives.** Upwork shows payment-verified, hire rate, total spend, live proposal count — facts about the *counterparty*, gathered by the platform.
8. **Exclusivity must be stated as a number, held as a constraint, and refunded if broken.** zolar: "sold to a maximum of 3 installers, never more."
9. **Outcome capture is the flywheel.** Without first-contact and won/lost writes, matching, reputation and refund adjudication are all unfalsifiable.
10. **Self-service cancel/pause.** Absence of it is the single most-cited billing complaint across Angi, CraftJack and Bark.

## TOP CANDIDATES FOR SOLARPRO

### 1. Lead Assurance — a stated, bounded, partly automatic refund policy
**Build:** a `POST /api/network/opportunities/[id]/dispute` route writing the already-existing `opportunity_assignments.dispute_filed_at` / `refund_*` columns; a 4-state tracker (Pending / Refunded / Credited / Denied) on the claimed-lead card; and a published policy page with an explicit **negative** list, Networx-style.
**Why it beats what we have:** SolarPro currently has *no* contractor-facing refund path at all. `dispute_filed_at` has zero writers. Thumbtack's policy is the industry's best and even it hides behind "sole discretion"; a bounded policy with an auto-refund tier beats it outright.
**Bounded by:** 7-day window; qualifying list = invalid/disconnected phone, wrong state, homeowner does not own the property, duplicate within 45 days. Everything else denied by default. Refund to Stripe, not to credit.
**Proof:** file a dispute on a test lead in Stripe test mode, see `dispute_filed_at` and `refund_status` move, see the tracker render, see the Stripe refund land.

### 2. Speed-to-Claim discount + the `first_contact_at` writer it forces
**Build:** the claimed-lead card's tel:/mailto: links write `first_contact_at` on first click; if the first contact is inside 30 minutes of `claimed_at`, issue a partial Stripe refund of 20% (CraftJack's mechanic, but as a rebate rather than a pre-discount, since SolarPro charges up front).
**Why it beats what we have:** SolarPro's exclusivity is worthless if the lead sits for two days. And this single change un-blanks the whole reputation stack — `first_contact_at`, `close_status` and `lost_reason` currently have **zero writers**, which makes `lib/intelligence/producers.ts` `contractor_performance` (response speed, close rate, dispute rate) structurally vacuous and makes `contractorMatcher`'s 20% Performance weight dead.
**Bounded by:** rebate only on exclusive leads; one per claim; cap at $15.
**Proof:** claim a test lead, click the phone link, assert `first_contact_at` set and a 20% Stripe refund created; assert the producer now emits a non-null `avg_response_hours`.

### 3. Verified Homeowner Contact badge
**Build:** phone validity lookup at intake (line type + reachability), stored on the lead; a "Phone Verified" badge in `deriveMarketplaceBadges`, with `reason`; auto-refund if a verified number is later reported dead.
**Why it beats what we have:** dead phone numbers are the single most-cited reason contractors abandon these platforms (Angi: "20–40% ghost/fake"). SolarPro does no phone verification at all today, while already shipping a *harder* signal — "Verified Bill" — that no comparator has. Pairing them is a category-leading trust story.
**Bounded by:** verification at intake only; **fail-closed** — an unverified number sells without the badge, never with it (see `nearmap-quota-incident`: metered externals never fail open).
**Proof:** a known-bad number must not earn the badge; the badge must appear on the card and in the checkout line item.

### 4. Autobuy + weekly spend cap
**Build:** `contractor_profiles` already carries service states, ZIPs, radius, ecosystems, min/max kW. Add `autobuy_enabled`, `autobuy_weekly_cap_cents`, `autobuy_max_price_cents`, `autobuy_min_grade`. On lead release, matching contractors are charged off a saved Stripe payment method until the weekly cap is hit.
**Why it beats what we have:** today an installer must sit on the county map to catch a lead. zolar ships exactly this ("Matching leads are bought the instant they land") and it is the difference between a browsing toy and a supply channel.
**Bounded by:** opt-in; hard weekly cap; one auto-buy per lead; instant kill switch; every auto-buy emails a receipt with a one-click dispute link (feeds candidate 1).
**Proof:** release a test lead in a contractor's territory, assert one charge, assert the second release past the cap does not charge.

### 5. First-Claim Guarantee
**Build:** a contractor's **first** lead purchase is fully refundable for 14 days, no reason required, one per account — Bark's Get Hired Guarantee, minus Bark's Apple-Pay escape hatch.
**Why it beats what we have:** SolarPro's hardest dollar is an unknown installer's first $75 on an unknown marketplace. The auto-refund on a lost claim race already proves the platform can refund without a human.
**Bounded by:** first purchase only; 14 days; automatic; the lead returns to inventory on refund.
**Proof:** buy as a fresh contractor, request the guarantee, assert Stripe refund + lead relisted + flag set so it cannot be used twice.

### 6. "What we will never do" panel on `/network`
**Build:** a short, factual panel: no membership fee, no contract, no cancellation fee, one contractor per lead, contact revealed only after payment, county-level location before payment.
**Why it beats what we have:** all six are already true in the code (`claim_mode` defaults to `'exclusive'` in migration 072; the claim route returns 402 `PAYMENT_REQUIRED` for any priced lead; the discover feed withholds address/lat/lng) and **none of it is stated in the UI**. Every competitor's worst review is one of these six.
**Bounded by:** pure copy — no behaviour change. Must be re-verified against code before each release so it never becomes an FTC-style unsubstantiated claim.
**Proof:** a test asserting each of the six claims against the code path it describes, so the panel can never drift from reality.
**Blocker before shipping the copy:** the "county-level location before payment" claim is not yet true on the wire — the gated territory and preview endpoints still return `location_city`. Close that first, or drop that bullet.

## AUDIT OF SOLARPRO'S EQUIVALENT — what the code actually does today

**Surface.** `app/network/page.tsx` (Discover, gated — individual leads are not browsable; "Leads are revealed by area") → `app/network/territory/[state]/page.tsx` (state KPIs + category distributions + county rollups; `?county=FIPS` reveals a scannable listing of quality-only lead cards) → `app/network/lead/[id]/page.tsx` (full product page: Lead Score ring, signal tags, county-only map, 25-yr ROI chart, sticky exclusive-claim sidebar) → Stripe Checkout → contact unlocks. Admin side: `app/admin/network/page.tsx` + `app/api/admin/network/marketplace/route.ts` (actions: `update_price`, `release_from_intake`, `match_contractors`).

**Contractor-facing mutations — the entire API surface is six routes:** `PATCH /contractor-profile`, `POST /opportunities` (share), `POST /opportunities/[id]/checkout`, `POST /opportunities/[id]/claim`, `DELETE /opportunities/[id]/claim`, `PATCH /opportunities/[id]`.

**Genuine strengths (better than the field):**
- `claim_mode` **defaults to `'exclusive'`** (`lib/migrations/072_marketplace_inventory_claim_v1.sql:25`), enforced atomically at webhook finalize with an auto-refund on a lost race (`lib/network/leadPurchase.ts:270-307`). Angi/CraftJack/Thumbtack all resell 3–6 ways.
- Every badge carries an evidence `reason` string (`lib/network/marketplaceBadges.ts`) — explainable trust signals, including **Verified Bill** and **Bill Parsed**, which no comparator ships.
- Per-row `evaluateContractorEligibility` on the discover feed, with a `[MARKETPLACE VISIBILITY DENIED]` log carrying denials and reasons — precisely the geo/capability match the FTC found HomeAdvisor did not honour.
- No membership fee, no contract, no termination fee. One-time Stripe charge.
- Pre-purchase privacy is largely real: the feed selects city/state/zip and explicitly comments `DO NOT expose: address, lat, lng…`; neither the county listing nor the lead product page renders city. **Caveat found during this audit:** `app/api/network/territory/[state]/route.ts:303` and `app/api/network/opportunities/[id]/preview/route.ts:47` both still return `location_city AS city` in the gated, pre-payment JSON. The UI drops it; the wire does not. Any authenticated contractor reading the response gets the city for free. Small, cheap to close, and it undercuts candidate 6's claim that location is county-only before payment.

**Confirmed defects — three vacuous-metric findings (same class as `regex-backslash-b-becomes-backspace`):**
1. `opportunity_assignments.dispute_filed_at` (migrations 051, 067) is **read** by `lib/intelligence/producers.ts:230` to compute contractor dispute rate, and is **never written** anywhere in `app/` or `lib/`. Dispute rate is structurally always 0.
2. `close_status`, `lost_reason`, `first_contact_at` likewise have **zero writers**. The `contractor_performance` producer's response-speed and close-rate observations are therefore derived from permanently-null columns.
3. `lib/network/contractorMatcher.ts:295` and `app/api/admin/network/contractor-match/[id]/route.ts:71` both hardcode `NULL::numeric AS avg_rating` — there is no `avg_rating` column in `contractor_profiles` (migration 044 defines only `avg_close_rate_pct` and `avg_response_hours`, and **neither is ever written**). The matcher's 20%-weighted Performance factor and its `highly_rated` reason are unreachable for every contractor.
